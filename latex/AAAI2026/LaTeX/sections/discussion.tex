\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Models come closest to human blind priors on free-text semantic similarity ($\sHM$), where the majority of LMs and several VLMs fall within the human LOO 95\,\% CI.
Yes/no alignment is partial: a small number of models reach within the CI, but most LMs are over-concentrated (below the CI lower bound), reflecting systematic presence bias rather than human-like uncertainty.
Count-distribution alignment fails across the board: the universal zero-default places every model above the CI upper bound, with no model approaching the human count distribution regardless of architecture or scale.
The sharpest divergence is on abstention.
Participants confronting the blind condition without instruction overwhelmingly declined to answer---the image-grounded task becomes semantically undefined without the image---whereas model blind abstention rates are universally low (0--17\%), even though the questions were drawn from linguistically predictable VQA items where corpus statistics partially support an answer.
Under the explicit instruction that no image is present, some models do reach near-zero abstention (LLaVA-1.5 and LLaVA-Vicuna~(LM): ${\approx}0.6\%$), superficially approaching human compliance.
However, for these models this reflects a stable low-abstention prior rather than genuine instruction sensitivity: their blind abstention was already near zero before any instruction.
The exception is InternVL~(LM), which drops from $17.1\%$ to $9.5\%$ under instruction---a real behavioral shift---while simultaneously ranking among the top models on distributional alignment ($\djs$), semantic similarity ($\sHM$), and difficulty tracking ($\rho$), making it the most coherently aligned model across all four criteria.
This is not a capability gap but a training one: anti-hallucination objectives penalize uncertainty expression, locking models into a single confident-generation regime even when confidence is unwarranted.

This raises a question worth pursuing: \emph{should} models align with humans on abstention?
The human behavior observed here is not mere caution---it is \emph{context-switching}.
Humans modulate their epistemic mode to the task: answering confidently when grounded, speculating productively when prompted to imagine, abstaining when evidence is absent.
Current models lack this toggle.
We suggest that better alignment requires not removing safety constraints but adding deliberate modes---a grounded mode that surfaces uncertainty when evidence is absent, and an explicitly licensed creative mode where confident prior-driven generation is appropriate.
Whether such a distinction can be learned, instructed, or enforced architecturally is an open question that the human--model divergence documented here makes concrete.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Participants were recruited from university communities, limiting demographic scope.
Model evaluation uses greedy decoding, which may underestimate answer diversity, which should be explored in future research.
Human data covers only the instruction-blind condition; corpus-frequency correlation
\citep{mccoy2024embers} and broader image ablations are similarly left to future work.
Although benchmark contamination may also amplify blind performance (see \citet{chen2024are} show that VLMs can achieve non-trivial scores without visual input partly due to training-set leakage), we stress that our pronominalization test partially controls for this, since models that rely on memorized question--answer pairs should degrade more sharply when content nouns are removed.
